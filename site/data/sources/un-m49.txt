statistical use (M49) This is the online version of the United Nations publication "Standard Country or Area Codes for Statistical Use" originally published as Series M, No. 49 and now commonly referred to as the M49 standard. The print version of the standard was issued last in 1999 and previously in 1996 , 1982 , 1975 and 1970 . M49 is prepared by the Statistics Division of the United Nations Secretariat primarily for use in its publications and databases. The assignment of countries or areas to specific groupings is for statistical convenience and does not imply any assumption regarding political or other affiliation of countries or territories by the United Nations. Questions & Answers Q: Why is the "Standard country or area codes for statistical use" known as M49 A: In 1970, the UN Statistical Office (as the United Nations Statistics Division was then known) published a document on "United Nations Standard Country Code", this was catalogued as Series M, No. 49. As a result, subsequent revisions of this document became known as M49. Q: How and when does M49 get revised? A: Historically, four revisions were published since the first publication in 1970. Now any updates are published continuously through the website and in Annex I of the United Nations Statistical Yearbook showing a snap shot of the M49 list as of 31 July each year. Changes to name of the countries and areas are more frequent than changes of codes, as the latter requires a significant change to the statistical territory of that country or area. Q: How do changes of the country or area names occur in M49? A: Name changes are usually requested by a United Nations (UN) Member State through UN Protocol. The set of standard names can be found on the UNTerm website . The M49 list adopts the official short name shown on UNTerm. Q: How do changes in the country or area codes occur in M49? A: The introduction or retirement of a code within M49 is often the result of new United Nations General Assembly or Security Council resolutions or at least with the express permission of the United Nations Member State(s) involved. Retired codes are usually appended with the text "[former]". Q: Are there any reserved codes for users’ own purposes within the M49? A: Yes, the code range between 900 and 999 (inclusive) are available for individual users for their own purposes, for example to designate countries or areas and groupings not shown in on this website or in the four revision publications (1970, 1975, 1982, 1996 or 1999) issued in the past. Q: What is the difference between the lettered codes (i.e. Afghanistan = AFG) and the numerical codes (i.e. Afghanistan = 004)? A: The lettered codes are maintained by the International Organization for Standardization (ISO) as part of their ISO3166-1 standard. Both 2-letter and 3-letter codes are maintained for each country or area. Their purpose is to represent the "name" identity of the country or territory. The numerical codes are maintained by the United Nations Statistics Division as part of the Standard country or area codes for statistical use (M49). Their purpose is to represent the "statistical" identity. In aggregate the numerical codes represent a mutually exclusive set of the statistical geographies of the world. Q: When does the lettered code (i.e. Afghanistan = AFG) change for a country or area, but the numerical code (i.e. Afghanistan = 004) does not change ? A: In cases where the "name" identity of a country or area changes but the "statistical" identity does not change; the lettered code will change but the numerical code will not change as in the following examples: M49 Code ISO-3 code ISO-2 code Name (until year changes) 104 BUR BU Burma (until 1989) 104 MMR MM Myanmar 180 ZAR ZR Zaire (until 1997) 180 COD CD Democratic Republic of the Congo 626 TMP TP East Timor (until 2002) 626 TLS TL Timor-Leste Q: When does the lettered code (i.e. Afghanistan = AFG) not change for a country or area, but the numerical code (i.e. Afghanistan = 004) does change ? A: In cases where the "name" identity of a country or area does not change but the "statistical" identity does change; the lettered code will not change but the numerical code will change as in the following examples: M49 Code ISO-3 code ISO-2 code Name (see M49 website for explanation of changes) 280 DEU DE Germany, Federal Republic of [former] 276 DEU DE Germany 230 ETH ET Ethiopia [former] 231 ETH ET Ethiopia 736 SDN SD Sudan [former] 729 SDN SD Sudan Q: When does both the lettered code (i.e. Afghanistan = AFG) and the numerical code (i.e. Afghanistan = 004) change for a country or area? A: In cases where both the "name" and "statistical" identity of a country or area change; this usually means the new entity is a completely new country or area within the list; for example, South Sudan. Q: How was the country and area composition of the geographic regions presented in M49 decided? A: The geographical regional schema was first presented under the 2 nd revision of the M49 (1982) based on continental regions; further subdivided into sub-regions and intermediary regions drawn as to obtain greater homogeneity in sizes of population, demographic circumstances and accuracy of demographic statistics. The regional composition of Africa and Americas remain unchanged from that time. The regions of Europe and Asia were extended in 3 rd revision (1996) with the new countries resulting from the break-up of the Union of Soviet Socialist Republics (USSR), which was a region in itself in the original schema. The Oceania region was reorganised in the 4 th revision (1999) following the cessation of the Pacific Islands (Trust Territory). Q: What's the difference between North America and Northern America in M49? A: North America (numerical code 003) contains Northern America (numerical code 021), the Caribbean (numerical code 029) and Central America (numerical code 013). Q: Why is Mexico not part of Northern America? A: While Mexico is not part of Northern America (numerical code 021), it is part of North America (numerical code 003). For the difference between Northern America and North America in M49, see the answer to the previous question. Q: How was the country and area composition of the Least Developed Countries (LDCs), the Land Locked Developing Countries (LLDCs) and the Small Island Developing States (SIDS) decided? A: The group of Least Developed Countries (LDC) are defined through a formal process by the Department of Economic and Social Affairs' Economic Analysis and Policy Division. There is no common UN wide definition of the countries and areas which are considered Land Locked Developing Countries (LLDCs) or Small Island Developing States (SIDS). Instead the current composition of countries and areas in LLDCs and SIDS groups were set at the beginning of the Sustainable Development Goals reporting period based on various definitions available at the time. Q: Why is Kosovo not shown in the M49? A: The status of Kosovo should be understood to be in the context of United Nations Security Council resolution 1244 (1999). As a result, within the "Standard country or area codes for statistical use (M49)", Kosovo is currently considered part of Serbia (numerical code 688). However, for strictly statistical purposes, the numerical code 412 can be used to represent this area. Q: Why is Taiwan Province of China not shown in the M49? A: On the 25 th October 1971, the UN General Assembly adopted a resolution (2758) to recognize the representatives of the Government of the People's Republic of China as the only legitimate representatives of China to the United Nations. As a result, within the M49, Taiwan Province of China is considered part of China (numerical code 156). However, for strictly statistical purposes, the numerical code 158 can be used to represent this area. Q: What is the distinction of “Developed regions” and “Developing regions”? A: There is no definition of developing and developed countries (or areas) within the UN system. However, in 1996 the distinction between “Developed regions” and “Developing regions” was introduced to the Standard country or area codes for statistical use (known as M49). These groupings were intended solely for statistical convenience at the time and did not express a judgement about any country’ or area’s stage of development. Over time the use of the distinction between "Developed regions" and "Developing regions", including in the flagship publications of the United Nations, has diminished. Since 2017, the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs) report and the statistical annex to the Secretary General’s annual report on SDGs progress uses only geographic regions without referring to the two groupings of developed and developing regions. Therefore, following consultation with other international and supranational organizations active in official statistics, the “Developed regions” and “Developing regions” were removed from the "Other groupings" of the M49 in December 2021. Following its removal from M49, several users expressed the need to maintain the distinction of developed and developing regions based on the understanding that being part of either developed or developing region is through sovereign decision of a state. Therefore, a file was created that contains an updated classification of developed and developing regions as of May 2022, in addition to the historical classification of December 2021. See answer to question “Where do I find the classification of “Developed regions” and “Developing regions”?” The updated classification reflects a recent action by the Republic of Korea to be considered part of developed regions. Users may continue to use these historical and updated groupings if needed and in their specific contexts. They can also refer to the similar grouping of "developing countries" associated with specific SDG indicators where the exact composition of the group may vary, depending on the mandate, membership or analytical interest of the custodian agency responsible for the particular indicator or use other available groupings such as by income-level. Users can also construct their own groupings based on the current list of geographical regions or country codes maintained in the M49 pages.
